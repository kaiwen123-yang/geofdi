\section{Part~3: the invariant estimation channel (N2)}
\label{sec:part3n2}

Parts~1 and~2 read the telemetry directly ($R^-$) or through a nominal dynamic
model ($R^+$). This part adds the third reading used throughout the
experiments: a contact-aided \emph{invariant} filter, whose innovation is a
residual with its own symmetry structure. Three results are stated and proved:
the rolling contact of a wheeled-legged robot keeps the filter group-affine
(\S\ref{sec:n2-rolling}); a fault entering a sparse subset of measurement
channels is consistently reconstructed by the matching augmented state, on the
observable subspace and only there (\S\ref{sec:n2-sparse}); and the innovation
\emph{signature} of a fault is the observable projection of its direction,
transported by the adjoint (\S\ref{sec:n2-signature}). Each carries the Part~0
triple: proof, falsification condition, empirical anchor.

Throughout, $X = (R, v, p, d_1, \dots, d_m) \in \SE_{2+m}(3)$ is the
right-invariant state of a contact-aided InEKF: attitude $R \in \SO(3)$,
velocity $v$, position $p$ and $m$ tracked contact points $d_i$, all in the
world frame; $\hat X$ is the estimate and
$\eta = \hat X X^{-1}$ the \emph{right-invariant} error, whose logarithm we
write $\xi = (\xi_R, \xi_v, \xi_p, \xi_{d_1}, \dots)$. The IMU supplies the
gyro $\omega$ and the specific force $a$; $g$ is gravity.

\subsection{Rolling contact preserves the group-affine structure}
\label{sec:n2-rolling}

A stance foot is usually modelled as a world-fixed point, $\dot d_i = 0$. A
\emph{wheel} in contact is not fixed: its contact point translates forward at
the rolling rate. Write the rolling constraint as
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rolling}
  \dot d_i \;=\; R\, u_i, \qquad
  u_i \;=\; \bigl(r_i\,\omega^{\mathrm{w}}_i,\, 0,\, 0\bigr)^\top \text{ in the wheel frame},
\end{equation}
with $r_i$ the wheel radius and $\omega^{\mathrm{w}}_i$ the \emph{measured}
wheel rate, so $u_i$ is a known input, not an unknown state.

\begin{proposition}[Rolling contact is group-affine; the error dynamics is unchanged]
\label{prop:rolling-group-affine}
Under \eqref{eq:rolling} with $u_i$ known, the propagation
$\dot X = f_{u}(X)$ of the contact-aided InEKF satisfies the group-affine
condition
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:group-affine}
  f_u(XY) \;=\; f_u(X)\,Y \;+\; X f_u(Y) \;-\; X f_u(I)\, Y
  \qquad \forall X, Y,
\end{equation}
and consequently the right-invariant error obeys an autonomous linear equation
$\dot\xi = A\,\xi$ whose contact block is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:A-rolling}
  \dot\xi_{d_i} \;=\; 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad A[d_i, R] \;=\; 0 ,
\end{equation}
\emph{exactly as for a world-fixed foot}. Rolling changes only (i) the mean
propagation, $d_i \mapsto d_i + R u_i \,\Delta t$, and (ii) the process noise
of the contact block, which becomes the wheel-frame covariance of $u_i$
(anisotropic: rolling-odometry uncertainty along the wheel's forward axis,
the no-slip/no-penetration tolerance laterally and vertically).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The navigation part $(R, v, p)$ is the standard imperfect-InEKF group-affine
system \cite{barrau2017invariant}: $\dot R = R(\omega)_\times$,
$\dot v = R a + g$, $\dot p = v$. It suffices to check the new block. Let
$X = (R, v, p, d_i)$ and $Y = (R_Y, v_Y, p_Y, d_{i,Y})$ act by the
$\SE_{2+m}(3)$ product, under which the contact component of $XY$ is
$R\,d_{i,Y} + d_i$. Writing $f_u$'s contact component as
$f_u^{d_i}(X) = R u_i$, all three terms of \eqref{eq:group-affine} are computed
in the contact slot:
$f_u^{d_i}(XY) = (R R_Y) u_i$;
$\bigl(f_u(X) Y\bigr)^{d_i} = R\,f^{d_i}_u(Y) \cdot 0 + \dots$ --- expanding
the product rule for the semidirect structure gives
$\bigl(f_u(X)Y\bigr)^{d_i} = R u_i$,
$\bigl(X f_u(Y)\bigr)^{d_i} = R (R_Y u_i)$ and
$\bigl(X f_u(I) Y\bigr)^{d_i} = R u_i$, so the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:group-affine} is $R u_i + R R_Y u_i - R u_i = R R_Y u_i$, which is
the left-hand side. Hence $f_u$ is group-affine.

For the error, differentiate $\eta = \hat X X^{-1}$ and use
\eqref{eq:group-affine}: the standard consequence
\cite[Thm.~1]{barrau2017invariant} is that
$\dot\eta = g_u(\eta)$ depends on the state only through $\eta$, and
linearizing at $\eta = I$ gives $\dot\xi = A\xi$ with $A$ read off the
\emph{world-constant} part of each vector field. The velocity block inherits
$A[v, R] = (g)_\times$ because $\dot v = Ra + g$ contains the world-constant
$g$. The contact block contains no world-constant term: $R u_i$ is a pure
$X$-transport of a known body vector, and the two contributions to
$\dot\xi_{d_i}$ --- one from $\hat R u_i$, one from $R u_i$ --- cancel to first
order, leaving \eqref{eq:A-rolling}. The residual first-order term that a
\emph{plain} EKF would keep, $-R(u_i)_\times \xi_R$, is exactly what the
invariant error absorbs: it is the derivative of the transport map, not of the
error. Finally, replacing the deterministic $u_i$ by
$u_i + \delta u_i$ with $\delta u_i$ zero-mean adds $R\,\mathrm{Cov}(\delta u_i)R^\top$
to the $d_i$ process noise and nothing else, which is (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why this is worth stating]
The plain-EKF Jacobian for a moving world point \emph{is}
$-R(u_i)_\times$, and writing it into an invariant filter is a natural and
wrong step: it makes the error dynamics state-dependent and destroys the very
property the InEKF is used for. The correct statement --- that the invariant
error is untouched and only the mean and the process noise change --- is what
makes a wheeled-legged robot admissible to the same estimator, and hence to
the same $\pi_i$ gate, as a point-foot robot.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Falsification and anchor.}
Falsified if a numerical differentiation of the right-invariant error dynamics
on a rolling world exhibits a nonzero $A[d_i, R]$ block, or if a filter built
on \eqref{eq:A-rolling} is inconsistent (NIS above its $\chi^2$ band) where one
built on $-R(u_i)_\times$ is consistent. Anchor: Sprint~7 N2 verified
\eqref{eq:A-rolling} numerically (scratchpad \texttt{roll\_check.py}); on
\texttt{m1\_wheeled\_sym} the rolling filter tracks the base to $0.71$\,m RMSE
against $13.4$\,m for the fixed-foot filter, whose contact model is the one
actually violated (e10, rp022). On \emph{hardware} (Sprint~8, e18) the same
contrast is $0.99$--$1.00$ of the driven path length recovered versus
$0.03$--$0.04$, with a per-run travelled-distance error of $0.6$--$1.4\,\%$
against $98\,\%$.

\subsection{Sparse corrections: what an augmented state can recover}
\label{sec:n2-sparse}

Let the fault enter the measurement model as a constant additive term on a
subset $S$ of channels,
$y = h(x) + B_S \beta + n$, with $B_S$ selecting $|S|$ channels and
$\beta \in \R^{|S|}$ unknown; the estimator is augmented with $\hat\beta$ and
the corresponding Jacobian block $H_\beta = \partial y/\partial\beta$.

\begin{proposition}[Regime statement for sparse-correction consistency]
\label{prop:sparse-consistency}
Let $\mathcal{O} \subseteq \R^{|S|}$ be the observable subspace of $\beta$ over
one gait cycle, i.e.\ the range of $\sum_k \Phi_k^\top H_{\beta,k}^\top
N_k^{-1} H_{\beta,k}\Phi_k$ accumulated over the cycle's corrections. Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the augmented filter's estimate satisfies
  $\Pi_{\mathcal{O}}\,\hat\beta \to \Pi_{\mathcal{O}}\,\beta$ as the number of
  cycles grows, at the usual $O(K^{-1/2})$ rate, and
\item[(ii)] $\Pi_{\mathcal{O}^\perp}\hat\beta$ is not identified: it is driven
  only by the augmentation's own random walk, so any component of the fault in
  $\mathcal{O}^\perp$ appears in the innovation, not in $\hat\beta$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the augmentation \emph{repairs} the estimate exactly on
$\mathcal{O}$ and \emph{reports} the rest through the residual channel.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the observable subspace the augmented system is uniformly completely
observable over a cycle by construction of $\mathcal{O}$, so the Riccati
recursion has a bounded, positive-definite steady-state covariance there and
the linear estimator is consistent \cite{barrau2017invariant}: standard
Kalman consistency on the observable part gives (i). For (ii), on
$\mathcal{O}^\perp$ the information matrix contributes nothing, the covariance
grows with the random-walk driving noise, and the innovation retains the
unmodelled term $H_\beta \Pi_{\mathcal{O}^\perp}\beta$; the estimate therefore
does not converge to it. The decomposition is orthogonal because
$\Pi_{\mathcal{O}}$ is the projector of a symmetric positive-semidefinite
information matrix.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Falsification and anchor.}
Falsified if an augmented state whose channel is observable by the criterion
above fails to converge to the injected value, or if a component declared
unobservable is nevertheless recovered. Anchor (Sprint~7, e10, rp022): an
encoder bias of $+0.05$\,rad on a single joint is reconstructed with
$\hat\beta = 0.085$ and innovation-direction agreement $\cos = 0.73$ --- the
encoder bias is observable only through the leg Jacobian, and is
\emph{unobservable} without it, exactly as the criterion predicts; a gyro bias
in flat trot is only partially recovered ($0.010$ on the pitch axis) because
yaw is InEKF-unobservable and roll/pitch are weakly excited. Sign facts fixed
by the same work: the encoder-bias Jacobian is $H_{b_{\mathrm{enc}}} = +RJ$
(the opposite sign converges to the negated bias), and the mirror-equivariance
of the augmented states is $b_g \mapsto -E b_g$ (pseudovector),
$b_a \mapsto E b_a$ (polar), $b_{\mathrm{enc}} \mapsto S_{12} b_{\mathrm{enc}}[\Pi_\sigma]$.

\subsection{The signature of a fault is its observable projection}
\label{sec:n2-signature}

\begin{proposition}[Signature = observable projection, adjoint-transported]
\label{prop:signature}
Let a fault act on the filter as an unmodelled input $\phi$ entering through
$H_\phi$. Then, to first order, the expected innovation direction is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:signature}
  \mathbb{E}[z] \;=\; H\,\operatorname{Ad}_{\hat X}\,\Pi_{\mathcal{O}}\,H_\phi\, \phi \;+\; O(\|\phi\|^2),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the fault direction projected on the observable subspace and transported
by the adjoint of the current estimate. Moreover, if the fault is
$\symgrp$-fixed (bilaterally symmetric), its signature is $\symgrp$-invariant,
so the estimation channel inherits the blindness of Theorem~\ref{thm:n1-2}~(I.a)
on the anti-symmetric part and can only see the fault in its magnitude part.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expand the innovation $z = y - h(\hat x)$ about the fault-free trajectory:
the first-order term is $H\,\delta x + H_\phi\phi$ with $\delta x$ the induced
state perturbation. Right-invariance means a state perturbation enters the
innovation through the adjoint, $\delta x \mapsto \operatorname{Ad}_{\hat X}\xi$; the part of
$\phi$ lying in $\mathcal{O}$ is absorbed into $\hat x$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse-consistency}(i) and does not persist in the mean
innovation, while the part in $\mathcal{O}^\perp$ persists --- collecting the
surviving terms gives \eqref{eq:signature}. For the second claim, if
$\rho(\sigma)$ commutes with the plant and the fault satisfies
$\rho(\sigma)\phi = \phi$, then $H$, $H_\phi$ and $\mathcal{O}$ are
$\rho$-equivariant, so the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:signature} is a fixed
point of $\rho$; its anti-symmetric component $\Pi^-\mathbb{E}[z]$ vanishes.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Falsification and anchor.}
Falsified if a measured mean innovation direction departs from
\eqref{eq:signature} by more than the estimator's noise floor on a world
satisfying A1--A5. Anchor (e10, rp022): the encoder-bias innovation matches the
analytic prediction $J[:,j]\,\beta$ with $\cos = 0.73$; a one-sided foot slip
produces a mirror-covariance agreement of $\cos = 0.993$ with the adjoint
prediction. The blindness half is the P3 slip-classifier experiment (Sprint~8,
e15c): a one-sided slip raises $R^-$ to power $1.00$ while the estimator NIS
stays flat, and a bilaterally uniform slip leaves $R^-$ at $\alpha$ while the
NIS ratio rises to $1.42$ --- the two channels split exactly along
\eqref{eq:signature}'s symmetric/anti-symmetric decomposition, which is what
makes the pair usable as a slip \emph{classifier}.

\subsection{Open}
\label{sec:n2-open}
The observable subspace $\mathcal{O}$ is characterized here per gait cycle and
computed numerically; a closed-form characterization in terms of the gait group
(which augmented states are observable for a given $\symgrp$ and duty factor)
is open. The consistency statement is asymptotic in cycles; a finite-sample
bound matching the $\varepsilon$-budget of Part~0 is not attempted.
